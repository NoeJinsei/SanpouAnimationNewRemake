Two things in the last scene were to be quicker: the clouds, a bit; and the plane as it leaves the runway, gathering speed the way a real one does. Nothing else in the last scene changed, and the 52-second loop is intact.

2. The plane builds speed from the start of its roll

What it did. It reached 95 px/s on the runway, then slowed to 74 as it left the ground and held that for nearly nine seconds, and only gathered speed near the top of the picture. That steady crawl up the sky is what looked wrong.

What it does now. On the runway itself it is as quick as it was (the roll is short and was already a strong push). The gain is in the climb. Its speed only ever rises, from the moment the brakes come off until it leaves the picture: a strong push down the runway, easing as the nose comes up, still building all the way up the climb, and building fastest once it is past Tokyo Tower.

What did not change

  • Its path. It climbs the same line and passes in front of the thin mast above Tokyo Tower's top deck, exactly where it did.
  • Its 26-second round, and when it passes the Tower. The quicker climb saves 2.8 s; the plane spends them standing lined up on the runway before it rolls (4.2 s, it was 1.4 s). So a flock still crosses the Tower nine seconds ahead of it, and the kite is still at the far end of its circle when it comes by.
  • The landing, the stop and everything on the ground.
  • No sudden change of speed anywhere: the push eases and builds smoothly.

What it costs on a phone

A phone only sees a narrow strip of the last scene, and its only open sky is the patch between the two buttons and the roofs, left of Tokyo Tower. Round 6 slowed the plane on purpose so that it would be seen there for longer. A faster plane is seen there for less time.

If 3 s feels too short on a phone, the climb can be made a little gentler through that patch without giving up the build-up: say so and I will show it.
