The plane, before and now

From the moment its wheels leave the runway to the moment it passes Tokyo Tower it now takes 6.3 seconds; it took 7.3. It is about a sixth faster through the middle of the climb and a little under a tenth faster at both ends, and its speed still only ever rises. The push along the runway itself is the same as before; the gain begins as the nose comes up.

What did not change

  • The clouds. They are as you saw them.
  • The plane's path. It climbs the same line and passes in front of the thin mast above Tokyo Tower's top deck, exactly where it did.
  • Its 26-second round, and when it passes the Tower. The quicker climb saves 1.1 s; the plane spends it standing lined up on the runway before it rolls (5.4 s, it was 4.2 s). So a flock still crosses the Tower ten seconds ahead of it, and the kite is still at the far end of its circle when it comes by.
  • The landing, the stop and everything on the ground.
  • No sudden change of speed anywhere: the push eases and builds smoothly, as before.
