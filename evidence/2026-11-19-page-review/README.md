# All 21 pages reviewed against the references (2026-11-19)

Seven readers, one group of pages each, every finding marked **CONVENTION** (match what a Japanese buyer
expects) or **CHARACTER** (look like this company, not like a competitor), and every one checked against
the site's own `COPY-STYLE-ja.md`, `WORDING-CHANGES-2026-11.md` and `CLIENT-QUESTIONS-ja.md` so nothing
already decided is proposed twice. **79 findings.**

## The verdict

Plain answer: this site is already better than most Japanese logistics sites of its size, and it does not need a redesign. It needs about fifteen small text and ordering fixes, and one decision from the client.

What is already strong, and this is not politeness. The site is precise where the industry is vague: real measured numbers with both units (990㎡（300坪）, 軒高4m以上, 全長18mのトレーラーが乗り入れ可能な荷捌きヤード), a fleet proved by a per-type count table, and limits stated out loud (「新潟県は新潟市方面のみ」) instead of a prefecture painted in. It makes no claim a reader could catch it out on: no No.1, no ソリューション, no equipment the company does not have. Unfinished sections are left hidden rather than filled with invented text, and one block that had been filled with sample data was found and deleted. Every link says where it goes — not one 「こちら」 — and all the deep links resolve. The enquiry form has a confirmation step, returns a reference number, and has a different error message for each kind of failure; most comparable forms have none of these. Three things have no equal anywhere in the reference material at all: the interactive coverage map, the 3PL scope diagram, and the receiving page (入庫のご案内) with its live opening-hours clock that knows the public holidays. The Japanese is plain and consistent, and the illustrations are honestly labelled as illustrations.

Where it falls short, in one sentence: in a few places it behaves like a consumer site rather than a business one, it prints the same fact in three or four places (and one of those copies has now gone wrong), and the one thing that makes this company different from every competitor is almost invisible.

The one real mistake. The warehouse opening hours are published on three pages and two of them are wrong. receiving.html says the lunch break is Wednesday and Thursday only; terms.html and network.html print the hours with no note, which reads as a break every weekday. terms.html is the legally posted notice page, so the version a careful customer or an inspector would trust is the incorrect one. Fix this first.

The missed opportunity. Paper on rolls is real work of theirs: four roll clamps across two depots, a 5m-high warehouse, an 18m trailer yard, a 270㎡ processing room, and 原紙（平判・巻取紙）in their own list of goods carried. On the whole 21-page site, the word ロールクランプ appears on two pages only — as two table cells and one tag — and exactly one sentence anywhere explains what it is for, on the vehicles page. The word 紙 does not appear on the home page at all. No competitor in the gathered research advertises roll handling, so this is the one thing on the site that cannot be copied from anybody. It is currently three words.

One conflict between the seven readers I resolved for you. Five different readers wanted the warehouse measurements added to five different pages (home, business, reasons, company, network). If all five were done, the same table would be in six places and would go stale the same way the hours just did. My ruling: fleet.html keeps the full spec tables, business.html#warehouse gets a short two-line summary because that is where the customer decides, the home page gets only the licence and 常温, and the other pages link instead of repeating.

What I dropped, and why. Four ideas from the readers were really "copy company X", so they are not in the character list: photographing each vehicle type the way one competitor does (their count table is stronger evidence, and photographs are already on the client's list); splitting the services into one page each like two competitors (a third keeps a single page, so it is not an expectation, and splitting would break the 3PL diagram); a separate 「ご家族の皆様へ」 page (only one of three comparable carriers has one, and its contents are nearly the same list this site already carries, so copying it would produce a duplicate — I kept it only as a block built from their own facts); and a row of certification badges (they hold one real credential; a thin badge row advertises the gap).

Scale and money. Almost everything ranked below is editing text or moving blocks — no agency, no developer beyond one small job on the contact form's show/hide. Two items need pictures. They do not need a photographer: the depot gate, the yard with a trailer in it, a roll in the clamp, and a daily vehicle check are all phone-camera shots on a clear day. The redrawn illustrations can come from the illustration style the company already owns from its hero animation.

## What already works, and must not be lost in a later pass

- Precision instead of adjectives. Real measured numbers with both units everywhere (990㎡（300坪）, 軒高4m以上／5m以上, a yard specified by the vehicle it takes), and limits stated out loud — the map legend says 「新潟県は新潟市方面のみ」 rather than colouring the whole prefecture. Round claims are the industry norm; precise ones are not, and a buyer trusts them more than any adjective.
- No overclaiming anywhere on 21 pages: no 'No.1', no ソリューション or 実現, no 必ず, and not one safety claim about equipment the company does not have. The restraint is deliberate and it is the hardest thing here to rebuild once lost — any later pass that wants a page to 'look stronger' will reach for exactly the claims that were correctly removed.
- Unfinished work is hidden, never filled with plausible text. Missing figures sit in hidden blocks with a note saying what is pending, and the one block that had been filled with sample data was found and deleted. The site also refuses to guess: the receiving clock shows 'please confirm' for the holiday seasons whose dates have not been published, instead of inventing them.
- The 3PL scope diagram. It draws the boundary of the service — the customer on one side, the delivery point on the other, four named steps between, and the company named across the middle — and grounds it in the two real warehouses and 32 own vehicles. Nothing comparable exists in any reference site. Do not let a later pass flatten it into a bullet list.
- Every link says where it goes. Not one 「こちら」 anywhere in the page content, and all the deep links resolve. Generic 'click here' links are everywhere on sites of this size; a redesign must not turn these back into buttons.
- The enquiry form is better than every reference form: a confirmation step, a reference number on completion, a different message for each kind of failure (including an honest one for a timeout, telling the customer to phone before resending), the consent text on the page rather than behind a link, and the application form deliberately lightened for drivers — phone required, e-mail optional, no home address asked for.
- The interactive coverage map and the receiving page have no equal in any of the gathered research. The map answers 'do you cover me' per prefecture, works by keyboard, credits its source, and hands the reader's own answer to the enquiry form. The receiving page publishes a real yard's rules in public, including the unflattering ones about waiting.
- Honest pictures and honest credits. Every illustration is labelled as an illustration, every borrowed photograph carries its photographer and licence, and the alt text describes what is actually drawn. That is better documented than any site in the research.
- Contact is reachable from everywhere and each number says what it is for, with reception hours attached and fax numbers for all three depots — this industry still uses fax, and most comparable sites give only the head office.
- Underneath, the build is careful: breadcrumbs, proper table headings, a skip link, hidden captions for screen readers, line-break control so names and numbers do not split awkwardly on a 390px phone, the mobile reading order fixed deliberately in the stylesheet, and an empty-state for the news list that cannot break when someone adds an article. Whoever maintains this after handover can do it.
